# graham-capstone


The Dataset.xlsx file contains the coded (and reverse-scored) data for this analysis, including participant numbers, the item scores for the three measures of interest, and four demographic variables. The details of the scale-based measures and their scoring are described below.

Experiences of Sexual Objectification (adapted from Cary et al. (2024); Kozee et al. (2007); Swim et al., (1998))
Instructions: Please use the following scale to indicate how often during the previous semester you experienced each of the events listed below. These events may have taken place in-person and/or online. (1 = Never, 2 = About once a semester, 3 = About once a month, 4 = About once a week, 5 = Two or more times a week)
1.	Experienced unwanted staring or ogling at myself or parts of my body when the person knew or should have known I was not interested or it was inappropriate for the situation or our relationship.	
2.	Experienced unwanted flirting when the person knew or should have known I was not interested or it was inappropriate for the situation or our relationship.	
3.	Felt that someone was staring at my body inappropriately.	
4.	Had sexist comments made about parts of my body or clothing.	
5.	Heard someone make comments about sexual behaviour I might do or things they would want to do with me.	
6.	Had someone do or say something that made me feel threatened sexually.	
7.	Had a romantic partner that seemed to be more interested in my body than in me as a person.	
8.	Received unwanted messages online asking me to send nude photos/videos or to describe my body.	
9.	Received unwanted messages online asking about my sexual experience/behaviours.	
10.	Had people shout sexist comments, whistle, or make catcalls at me.
11.	Encouraged to change my body shape (e.g. lose weight).
Note: Items #1, #2, #4, #5, #6, and #10 were taken from Swim and colleagues’ (1998) Daily Sexist Events Scale. Items, #3, #7, and #11 were taken from Kozee and colleagues’ (2007) Interpersonal Sexual Objectification Scale. Items #8 and #9 were taken from Cary and colleagues’ (2024) Online Sexual Objectification Experiences Scale. Items are averaged to compute a composite score.

Benevolent Sexism (from the Ambivalent Sexism Inventory – Short Form; Glick & Whitehead, 2010)
Instructions: Please indicate the degree to which you agree or disagree with each statement using the rating scale provided. (1 = Strongly disagree, 2 = Moderately disagree, 3 = Neither agree nor disagree, 4 = Moderately agree, 5 = Strongly agree)
1.	Many women have a quality of purity that few men possess.	
2.	Women should be cherished and protected by men.	
3.	Every man ought to have a woman whom he adores.	
4.	Men are incomplete without women.	
5.	Women, compared to men, tend to have a superior moral sensibility.	
6.	Men should be willing to sacrifice their own well being in order to provide financially for the women in their lives.
Note: Items are averaged to compute a composite score.

Personal Safety Anxiety and Vigilance Scale (Calogero et al., 2021)
Instructions: For each statement, please select the response that best matches your attitudes and experiences. (1 = Completely unlike me, 2 = Unlike me, 3 = Slightly unlike me, 4 = Neither unlike me or like me, 5 = Slightly like me, 6 = Like me, 7 = Completely like me)
1.	I feel nervous about my safety when I am alone.	
2.	I have thoughts about my safety when I notice someone checking out my body/body parts.	
3.	* I worry about my safety when I notice someone checking out my body/body parts.	
4.	I would feel uncomfortable walking alone on the street at night.	
5.	I check behind me when I am walking alone to see if someone is there.	
6.	When alone, past experiences where I have felt physically threatened enter my mind.	
7.	I share my fears about my personal safety with others.	
8.	In general, I do not worry about my safety in my day-to-day environment. (Reverse-coded)
Note: * In the validated version of the scale, item #3 was reverse-scored: “I never worry about my personal safety”. Here, it was replaced with a positively scored item, based on the recommendation of the scale’s creator. Items are averaged to compute a composite score.

Demographics:
The demographic measures included self-reported level of religiosity (1 = Not at all religious, 7 = Very religious), political orientation (1 = Very left-leaning, 7 = Very right-leaning), age (in years), and identity as a member of the LGBTQ2S+ community (1 = Yes, 0 = No).
